### **Don't make this mistake**

A lithium-ion battery's voltage does not decrease linearly with its state of charge.

Don't simply assume that 3.7 V means 50% and 4.2 V means 100%.

Use a chemistry-appropriate voltage-to-state-of-charge lookup curve, preferably with calibration against your actual battery.

Also:

- Measure ADC voltage against a multimeter.
- Average several ADC readings.
- Measure battery voltage under operating load.
- Account for voltage-divider tolerance.
- Implement low-battery warnings.
- Test whether the ESP32 ADC readings remain stable while BLE is active.

**My recommendation:** Complete battery telemetry, but don't advertise it as accurate fuel gauging. Call it estimated state of charge.

And remember: the battery divider alone does not establish safe battery charging or protection. Verify the actual charger/protection circuit and enclosure before wearing it.

## **6. Important technical issues a professor could catch**

I would prepare answers to these before your presentation.

| Potential question                  | What you need to establish                               |
| ----------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| Why 2 kHz?                          | Nyquist requirement relative to your front-end bandwidth |
| Why 100 ms RMS?                     | Responsiveness versus signal stability                   |
| Why 72–720 Hz?                      | Actual sensor bandwidth and its implications             |
| Why MVC normalisation?              | Within-person reference, not force measurement           |
| Why BLE instead of Wi-Fi?           | Power, bandwidth and connectivity trade-offs             |
| Why one channel?                    | Cost and complexity versus simultaneous measurement      |
| How do you handle motion artefacts? | Current limitations and tested mitigation                |
| How reliable is MVC?                | Repeatability across trials                              |
| How long does the battery last?     | Measured runtime                                         |
| What happens if BLE disconnects?    | Reconnection and data-integrity behaviour                |

One particularly important issue: your front end is described as having a fixed 72–720 Hz bandwidth, whereas conventional sEMG analysis often uses approximately 20–450 Hz.

You need to explain the consequences. You cannot simply claim conventional EMG spectral analysis or fatigue detection with your current hardware.

Also, your 2 kHz sampling frequency is theoretically reasonable for a 720 Hz upper cutoff, but that does not prove that your actual acquisition achieves 2 kHz consistently.
